# METRON protocol

## Spend lifecycle

`PROPOSED -> (ALLOW -> APPROVED | DENY -> DENIED | ESCALATE -> ESCALATED -> explicit human approval -> APPROVED) -> SUBMITTED -> EXECUTED | FAILED | UNKNOWN`

Every paid action gets an immutable Spend Intent first. It binds the inspected seller wallet, HTTP method, `exact` or `GatewayWalletBatched` payment scheme, service URL, approved maximum, and SHA-256 hash of the canonical request body. The inspected service must accept Arc Testnet, and the body must be identical at execution. Immediately before dispatch, Circle's no-payment estimate must still select the approved scheme on Arc Testnet with the approved seller and a price within the approved maximum. METRON then reads the current Circle Agent Wallet USDC balance and requires `balance >= approved_max_cost + minimum_wallet_balance`. Failure returns `METRON_MINIMUM_WALLET_BALANCE_VIOLATION` and dispatches no payment. Policy uses exact integer USDC atomic units. A model-provided expected value informs ROI but is not proof. Per-action, daily, available-budget, and minimum wallet balance constraints are deterministic. `UNKNOWN` is terminal for automatic dispatch; query authoritative Circle state before any further action.

`max_spend_per_action` is the autonomous authority threshold: exceeding it returns `ESCALATE`. A human may approve that specific intent, but approval rechecks and cannot bypass either absolute cap: `max_daily_spend` or available operating capital (seed operating budget plus earned operating capital). Both absolute caps are rechecked atomically when an approved intent reserves dispatch, so a stale approval cannot spend against another day's authorization. Exceeding either cap returns terminal `DENY`; `minimum_expected_roi_bps` below its threshold also returns `DENY`. `DENY` cannot be human-overridden. An `ALLOW` intent is automatically persisted as `APPROVED`; an `ESCALATE` intent must first receive persisted `spend-approve` approval. Only `APPROVED` intents may execute.

Confirmed invoice receipts add `amount * reinvestment_bps // 10000` to earned operating capital in the same SQLite transaction as payment reconciliation. Wallet funding, internal transfers, registry records, claims, and submissions do not increase earned capital. Later spend decisions use seed operating budget plus earned operating capital; per-action and daily limits continue to apply.

## Payment evidence and accounting

Circle evidence is normalized into Payment Evidence and linked to one Payment Entry. Direct x402 uses Circle's transaction history; Gateway nanopayments use the x402 transfer-by-ID API and remain pending until Circle reports `confirmed` or `completed`. Evidence references are unique and can be consumed once. Journals are generated by METRON rules and must balance exactly:

| Source | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| x402 acquisition | 5000 Customer Acquisition Expense | 1000 USDC Operating Cash |
| Posted invoice | 1100 Accounts Receivable | 4000 Revenue |
| Reconciled customer payment | 1000 USDC Operating Cash | 1100 Accounts Receivable |

Historical account `1010` lines may remain in old SQLite databases; they are legacy records and no new operation posts to that account.

## EconomicRegistry

`EconomicRegistry` is one shared, non-custodial contract per supported network/profile. The Circle operating wallet is the namespace and must be the transaction caller. It records immutable hashes and atomic amounts for locally reconciled spend and revenue; it does not transfer tokens, execute x402, determine business truth, or grant spend authority. This plugin build has no trusted registry address, hash-construction path, or recording operation, so onchain mirroring is `INACTIVE / DEFERRED`; no spend or revenue records are currently mirrored. Any later integration requires persisted idempotent state and independent reconciliation; registry errors cannot change locally reconciled payment/accounting state or trigger another economic payment.

## Deal and invoice gates

`minimum_deal` is the commercial authority boundary: a deal below it returns `ESCALATE` and requires a persisted human exception while `NEGOTIATING`, before customer acceptance. Custom or legal commercial terms must be marked with `custom_or_legal_terms: true` at deal creation and also require persisted human approval before acceptance; any non-payer-wallet field in the deal `terms` object triggers the same gate. These pre-acceptance approvals are distinct from the final approval after the customer explicitly accepts, and do not advance the deal. The required sequence is `PROSPECTING -> NEGOTIATING -> PENDING_HUMAN -> HUMAN_APPROVED | HUMAN_REJECTED`; `deal-accept` is valid only from `NEGOTIATING`, and generic `deal-update` cannot set acceptance or approval states. Only final `HUMAN_APPROVED` permits invoice creation, and invoice posting also checks the deal state. An invoice becomes `PAID` only in the same SQLite transaction that consumes matching, confirmed inbound USDC evidence and posts its cash/receivable journal. Inbound evidence must match the payer address as well as the operating wallet, configured chain, currency, direction, amount, and open invoice.
