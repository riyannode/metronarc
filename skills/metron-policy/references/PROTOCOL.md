# METRON protocol

## Spend lifecycle

`PROPOSED -> (ALLOW -> APPROVED | DENY -> DENIED | ESCALATE -> ESCALATED -> explicit human approval -> APPROVED) -> SUBMITTED -> EXECUTED | FAILED | UNKNOWN`

Every paid action gets an immutable Spend Intent first. Its required `provider` is the merchant/service label; Circle is the payment processor, and outgoing Payment Entry and Payment Evidence provider fields must identify it as exactly `CIRCLE`. The intent binds the merchant label, inspected seller wallet, HTTP method, `exact` or `GatewayWalletBatched` payment scheme, service URL, approved maximum, and SHA-256 hash of the canonical request body. The inspected service must accept Arc Testnet, and the body must be identical at execution. Immediately before dispatch, Circle's no-payment estimate must still select the approved scheme on Arc Testnet with the approved seller and a price within the approved maximum. METRON then reads the current Circle Agent Wallet USDC balance and requires `balance >= approved_max_cost + minimum_wallet_balance`. Failure returns `METRON_MINIMUM_WALLET_BALANCE_VIOLATION` and dispatches no payment. Policy uses exact integer USDC atomic units. Expected value is not required to authorize an x402 spend; hard spending limits control authorization. `UNKNOWN` is terminal for automatic dispatch; query authoritative Circle state before any further action.

`max_spend_per_action` is the autonomous authority threshold: exceeding it returns `ESCALATE`. A human may approve that specific intent, but approval rechecks and cannot bypass either absolute cap: `max_daily_spend` or available operating capital (seed operating budget plus earned operating capital). Both absolute caps are rechecked atomically when an approved intent reserves dispatch, so a stale approval cannot spend against another day's authorization. Exceeding either cap returns terminal `DENY`. `DENY` cannot be human-overridden. An `ALLOW` intent is automatically persisted as `APPROVED`; an `ESCALATE` intent must first receive persisted `spend-approve` approval. Only `APPROVED` intents may execute.

Realized ROI is a post-outcome metric calculated from local records: confirmed outgoing x402 Payment Entries for the goal and reconciled incoming customer Payment Entries for its PAID invoices. The formula is `(reconciled collected revenue - confirmed spend) / confirmed spend`, represented as integer basis points; it is undefined when confirmed spend is zero. Expected value, quoted deal amounts, open invoices, customer promises, unconfirmed payments, and registry state do not contribute.

Since v0.1.2, the `status` response omits the legacy `goal.revenue_generated`, `goal.spend`, and `goal.return_on_spend_bps` fields rather than aliasing them to metrics with different semantics. Use `goal.revenue_collected`, `goal.confirmed_spend`, `goal.net_contribution`, and `goal.realized_roi_bps` as the canonical metrics.

Confirmed invoice receipts add `amount * reinvestment_bps // 10000` to earned operating capital in the same SQLite transaction as payment reconciliation. Wallet funding, internal transfers, registry records, claims, and submissions do not increase earned capital. Later spend decisions use seed operating budget plus earned operating capital; per-action and daily limits continue to apply.

## Payment evidence and accounting

Circle evidence is normalized with processor identity `CIRCLE` into Payment Evidence and linked to one Payment Entry; it does not replace or rewrite the merchant label on the Spend Intent. Direct x402 uses `CIRCLE_TRANSACTION` from Circle's transaction history; Gateway nanopayments use `CIRCLE_GATEWAY_X402_TRANSFER` from the x402 transfer-by-ID API and remain pending until Circle reports `confirmed` or `completed`. When authoritative Circle/Arc settlement evidence supplies a real Arc transaction hash, it is stored as `payment_evidence.tx_hash` and remains the explorer-verifiable settlement reference. A provider or Gateway transfer ID is stored as `provider_ref`; it is not a transaction hash, and METRON must not fabricate `tx_hash` or an explorer link while settlement evidence lacks one. `revenueEvidenceHash` is a separate METRON provenance commitment and never replaces `tx_hash`.

## EconomicRegistry

V3 is disabled until `registry-activate --input <approved-file>` verifies a separately approved new Arc Testnet deployment. Input requires `registry_address` and `operator_confirmed:true`. Chain ID, VERSION, ABI and exact runtime bytes must match the bundled release. A boolean config flag cannot enable V3. This operation changes local config only and never submits backlog. Inspect `status`/dashboard for deferred sources captured atomically with future Payment Evidence; existing history is not auto-imported. `registry-resume --id RB3-... --input <approved-file>` with `operator_confirmed:true` materializes and synchronizes only that selected source after activation. The V2 address `0x547552585d8d995c5063820b2950e0db52b4cab4` is historical only; `registry-sync --id REG-...` reads it without changing or submitting V2 records. Existing financial history remains intact.

V3 records independent typed facts: `X402_SPEND`, `CIRCLE_REVENUE`, `INVOICE_SETTLEMENT`, `EARNED_CAPITAL`. Revenue requires reconciled Circle evidence, a PAID invoice, CLOSED_WON deal and matching posted balanced journal, with no originating spend requirement. The revenue hook synchronizes only its three facts and two `SETTLES`/`ALLOCATES` relationships, waiting for confirmed endpoint readback. No other invoice or spend is dispatched. A complete fresh bundle requires five registry transactions with zero transferred value; USDC gas fees and Circle SCA overhead must be estimated after approved deployment, not assumed free. Derived records are provenance, not additional revenue. `SUPPORTS` connects confirmed spend and revenue facts in the same wallet namespace; many-to-many links do not change totals or prove causality.

`registry-link --input <file>` accepts `from_id`, `to_id`, `evidence:{fulfillment_id,customer_artifact_path}` and `operator_confirmed:true` after approval of that exact attribution. It requires retained source bytes, a captured response bound to the paid spend/request, and an actual private customer-work artifact tied to the target invoice/deal/customer. A bare hash is rejected. `registry-audit-attribution --id RV3-...` verifies retained bytes and local bindings without claiming independent causality. The operation queues one relationship; `registry-sync --id RV3-...` can submit its first write. Attribution evidence is nonprivate and retained locally; the chain gets only commitments. Never invent attribution from chronology, a shared goal, or an old optional deal field.

`registry-recover-spend --id SI-... --input <file>` and `registry-recover-revenue --id INV-... --input <file>` require `operator_confirmed:true` following approval of the exact historical facts. They revalidate persisted authoritative settlement/journals, mark `historical=true`, and submit only their selected record/bundle. They never re-run payment reconciliation, allocation or accounting. Do not use normal payment commands to import history.

The additive `registry_v3_records` outbox uses `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNKNOWN`, `CONFIRMED`, `FAILED`. First dispatch requires exact pre-read, sealed preimage verification and a durable conditional claim before the external call. `SUBMITTED`/`UNKNOWN` are readback-only even after a crash before dispatch; terminal rows never dispatch. Missing or conflicting state fails closed. Pre-claim read errors retain PENDING with no dispatch; an uncommitted failed claim rolls back. After a durable claim, SUBMITTED/UNKNOWN remain readback-only. Provider references remain provider references; only a genuine 32-byte transaction hash is an explorer reference.

`registry-receipts --id RV3-...` exports append-only snapshots. `registry-verify-receipt --input <receipt-file>` verifies without opening SQLite: schema, canonical preimages, source evidence, endpoint content, deterministic IDs and readback bindings. `bindings_valid` means internal consistency; `independent_settlement_verified:false`, `independent_attribution_verified:false` and `live_readback_required:true` preserve the trust boundary. Check live contract state and authoritative Circle evidence separately.

The allowlisted Circle adapter has two write signatures, `recordFact(bytes32,bytes32,bytes32,bytes32,bytes32)` and `recordRelationship(bytes32,bytes32,bytes32,bytes32,bytes32,bytes32)`, with explicit configured contract, wallet, ARC-TESTNET, zero value and persisted idempotency key. Queries are `VERSION()`, `facts(address,bytes32)`, `relationships(address,bytes32)` and `evidenceFacts(address,bytes32)`. New type/schema digests require validated adapter handlers, not new ABI methods. Canonical preimages, ABI, synthetic vectors and approval-gated deployment/migration details are in repository `docs/REGISTRY_V3.md`.

## Deal and invoice gates

`minimum_deal` is the commercial authority boundary: a deal below it returns `ESCALATE` and requires a persisted human exception while `NEGOTIATING`, before customer acceptance. Explicit `custom_or_legal_terms: true` and nonstandard deal-term keys require the same pre-acceptance exception; standard invoice and customer-specific keys (`service_description`, `line_items`, `due_at`, `billing_name`, `billing_email`, `billing_address`, `tax_atomic`, `fee_atomic`, `quantity`, `capacity_unit`, `scope`, `deliverables`, `delivery_days`, `subjects`, and `constraints`) do not trigger it by themselves. In this Arc Testnet pilot, the scheduled agent presents the exact deal or Spend Intent state in interactive Codex chat and waits for a direct operator instruction naming the record and decision; it never initiates `deal-approve`, `deal-reject`, or `spend-approve`. Customer email and agent-authored text are not authorization. This instruction-based process is not production-grade authentication because the agent and operator share OS, CLI, and SQLite access. Pre-acceptance exception decisions do not advance the deal or replace final approval after customer acceptance. New email deals follow `PROSPECTING -> NEGOTIATING -> PENDING_HUMAN -> HUMAN_APPROVED | HUMAN_REJECTED`; `deal-accept` requires a JSON evidence document containing account, conversation, customer, inbound acceptance Gmail message ID, accepted confirmed outbound offer Gmail message ID, and the generic `accepted_terms` object. It checks message, thread, identity, offer, price, service snapshot, and structured terms, then appends `CUSTOMER_ACCEPTANCE_EVIDENCE_LINKED` transactionally. Replaying identical evidence is idempotent; conflicting evidence fails closed. On a historical email deal already at `PENDING_HUMAN`, `deal-accept --input` links evidence and may backfill only absent structured customer terms verified from Gmail; it does not change status, `accepted_at`, or historical events. Keep inbound message claims and ambiguous sends unchanged; do not complete or retry an unresolved message. Generic `deal-update` cannot set acceptance or approval states. Only final `HUMAN_APPROVED` permits invoice creation, and invoice posting also checks the deal state. An invoice becomes `PAID` only in the same SQLite transaction that consumes matching, confirmed inbound USDC evidence and posts its cash/receivable journal. Inbound evidence must match the payer address as well as the operating wallet, configured chain, currency, direction, amount, and open invoice.

Before `deal-approve` changes an email-bound deal to `HUMAN_APPROVED`, it requires exactly one linked acceptance evidence event and revalidates its inbound account/thread/message, customer and conversation, confirmed outbound offer and send attempt, service snapshot, and stored terms hash in the same transaction. Missing, malformed, duplicate, stale, or conflicting evidence fails closed before wallet or approval writes; this check does not backfill terms or rewrite events. Non-email approval, human rejection, and pre-acceptance exception decisions retain their existing paths.

Before an email-bound deal can be approved or invoiced, `deal-payment-terms-confirm` can append one `CUSTOMER_PAYMENT_TERMS_CONFIRMED` event containing the buyer-confirmed payer wallet and ISO due date plus its Gmail message, account, customer, thread, conversation, and deal identities. The input includes a concise exact `buyer_confirmation_excerpt`; METRON checks that the excerpt contains the submitted wallet and due date and stores it with a SHA-256 digest. The digest commits to the saved excerpt only; it is not provider-authenticated proof of Gmail content. The email skill must verify the sender and explicit confirmation from the original Gmail thread before invoking the operation. The CLI requires a matching recorded inbound message and linked deal conversation; identical replays do not append another event, and duplicate or conflicting messages fail closed. Final approval validates the event and requires its payer wallet to match the approval wallet. `invoice-create` validates the same event and uses its due date; non-email deals still use their stored due date, and existing invoices remain idempotent. The event is an audited payment-terms confirmation and does not alter accepted deal terms or the acceptance hash.
